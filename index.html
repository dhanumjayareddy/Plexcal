Glossary: how to read these numbers

Diffusion length Å
The typical distance excitation energy spreads through the protein before it is lost. One ångström is 0.1 nanometre, roughly the width of an atom. Comparing FRET with DET shows which mechanism carries energy farther in this structure.
Chromophore pairs count
A chromophore is a light-absorbing side chain: tryptophan, tyrosine or phenylalanine. A pair is any two of them close enough for energy to pass between them. More pairs means more routes for energy to travel.
Coherent pairs
Pairs where the coupling is strong enough that the excitation is shared by both rings in a wave-like way, instead of hopping randomly from one to the other. Coherent pairs move energy faster and more efficiently.
Coherent-pair fraction %

Coherent pairs divided by total pairs, times 100. Near 0% means energy mostly hops; near 100% means almost every connection is wave-like. It is a share of the pairs, not the probability for any single pair.

Example: 40 coherent ÷ 200 total × 100 = 20%

What is being measured

When a tryptophan, tyrosine or phenylalanine ring absorbs a photon it becomes excited. That energy need not stay put. It can hop to a neighbouring aromatic ring, then another, wandering through the protein until it is lost as fluorescence or heat.

The photoexcitation energy diffusion length is the typical distance that wandering excitation covers before it is lost. A longer length means the protein can carry excitation farther from where the light first landed.

FRET Förster resonance energy transfer

The excited ring acts like a tiny antenna and hands its energy to a neighbour through electromagnetic coupling, with no contact needed. The rate falls with the sixth power of distance, so it works across several ångströms and fades quickly beyond the Förster radius.

kFRET ∝ r−6

DET Dexter electron transfer

The two rings must nearly touch. Their electron clouds overlap and exchange the excitation directly. The rate falls exponentially with distance, so it matters at near-contact and vanishes within a few ångströms.

kDET ∝ e−2r/L
