Excitation energy transfer in proteins

How far does excitation energy travel through a protein?

Aromatic amino acids absorb ultraviolet light and can pass that energy to their neighbours. Plexcal reads a protein structure and measures how far the energy can spread, using two established transfer models side by side.

What is being measured

When a tryptophan, tyrosine or phenylalanine ring absorbs a photon, it becomes excited. That energy does not have to stay put. It can hop to a nearby aromatic ring, then another, wandering through the protein until it is lost as fluorescence or heat.

The photoexcitation energy diffusion length is the typical distance this wandering excitation covers before it is lost. A longer length means the protein can carry excitation farther from where light first landed.

FRET Förster resonance energy transfer

The excited ring acts like a tiny antenna and hands its energy to a neighbour through electromagnetic coupling, with no contact needed. The rate falls with the sixth power of distance, so it works over several ångströms and fades quickly beyond the Förster radius.

kFRET ∝ r−6

DET Dexter electron transfer

The two rings must nearly touch. Their electron clouds overlap and exchange the excitation directly. The rate falls exponentially with distance, so it dominates at very short range and vanishes almost completely by a few ångströms.

kDET ∝ e−2r/L

How to read these numbers

Diffusion length Å
The typical distance excitation energy spreads through the protein before it is lost. One ångström is 0.1 nanometre, about the width of a single atom. Compare FRET and DET to see which mechanism carries energy farther in this structure.
Chromophore pairs count
A chromophore is a light-absorbing side chain: tryptophan, tyrosine or phenylalanine. A pair is any two of them close enough for energy to pass between them. More pairs means more routes for energy to travel.
Coherent pairs
Pairs where the coupling is strong enough that the excitation is shared between both rings in a wave-like way, rather than hopping randomly from one to the other. Coherent pairs move energy faster and more efficiently.
Coherent-pair fraction %
Coherent pairs divided by total pairs, times 100. If 40 of 200 pairs are coherent, the fraction is 20%. Near 0% means energy mostly hops; near 100% means almost every connection is wave-like. It is a share of the pairs, not a probability for any single pair.
